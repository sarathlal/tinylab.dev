Just 20 companies can access OpenAI's most powerful new models — after the White House stepped in before launch. Meanwhile, Anthropic pushed into three corners of the enterprise stack, and June's AI market selloff erased $2.7 trillion from the Magnificent Seven.

## US Government Takes the Wheel on Frontier AI

### OpenAI's GPT-5.6 — Sol, Terra, and Luna — Releases to 20 Government-Approved Companies

Just 20 organizations can currently access OpenAI's new GPT-5.6 family, launched June 26. The White House asked OpenAI to restrict the rollout after assessing GPT-5.6's capabilities as "on par" with Anthropic's restricted Mythos model. Federal officials are approving each customer individually, with broader access promised "in the coming weeks." This is the first time the US government has preemptively asked an American AI lab to restrict a model launch before release.

The family has three tiers: Sol, OpenAI's strongest model yet with new capabilities in coding, biology, and cybersecurity, priced at $5 input / $30 output per million tokens; Terra, which matches GPT-5.5 performance at half the cost; and Luna, a fast affordable option at $1 / $6. A new "ultra" mode chains sub-agents for complex multi-step tasks. OpenAI pushed back: "It keeps the best tools from users, developers, enterprises, cyber defenders, and global partners who need them."

### Arizona, California, and Rhode Island All Make AI Moves in One Week

Arizona Governor Katie Hobbs vetoed three AI bills on June 19 — a chatbot disclosure requirement, a ban on AI-generated nonconsensual images, and a mandate for state agencies to adopt AI — as part of an 88-bill veto sweep in a single day. California's legislature sent a ban on AI public school teachers to Governor Newsom, who hasn't signed it yet. Rhode Island Governor McKee signed a chatbot therapy ban into law. Nationwide, 78 chatbot-related bills are alive in 27 states.

Federal inaction is driving this wave. Companies now face a patchwork of conflicting rules that shift by state line.

## Anthropic Moves Into the Enterprise Stack

### Claude Tag Becomes a Slack Coworker — and Already Writes 65% of Anthropic's Own Code

Claude Tag, Anthropic's new Slack tool, already handles 65% of Anthropic's own product team code through the internal version. Launched June 23, it adds @Claude to any Slack channel as a shared AI team member — multiplayer by design, meaning anyone in the channel can see what Claude is working on and pick up where a colleague left off. "Ambient mode" lets Claude proactively flag relevant updates from across the workspace and follow up on quiet threads. It's available in beta for Enterprise and Team customers, replacing the existing Claude in Slack app.

*Sources: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/23/anthropic-claude-tag-virtual-employee-tool-slack/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/anthropics-claude-tag-is-learning-your-company-one-slack-message-at-a-time/) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/claude-tag-embeds-anthropics-ai-in-slack-already-writes-65-percent-of-internal-code-company-says/)*

### Micron Invests in Anthropic's Series H and Signs a Multi-Year Memory Supply Deal

Micron hit a record close of $1,211.38 on June 22, the day it announced a strategic agreement with Anthropic. The deal covers high-bandwidth memory, DRAM, and SSD supply for Anthropic's AI training and inference workloads — plus a direct equity stake in Anthropic's Series H funding round. Anthropic has already deployed Claude models across Micron's engineering and manufacturing functions.

*Sources: [Micron](https://investors.micron.com/news-releases/news-release-details/micron-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-agreement-scale-next) · [Blocks and Files](https://www.blocksandfiles.com/ai-ml/2026/06/23/micron-invests-in-anthropic-and-grants-it-a-supply-deal/5260050) · [Investing News](https://investingnews.com/micron-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-agreement-to-scale-next-generation-ai-infrastructure/)*

### Project Glasswing Has Found 10,000 Critical Vulnerabilities and Just Expanded to 150 Organizations

Anthropic's Project Glasswing has uncovered more than 10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities since April using Claude Mythos Preview. Of those, 6,202 came from scanning over 1,000 open-source projects. The program expanded in early June to roughly 150 organizations in 15+ countries, adding healthcare, energy, and communications sectors. Anthropic committed $100M in model credits and $4M in cash donations to open-source security groups.

## Big Tech's $2.7 Trillion AI Bill Comes Due

### Magnificent Seven Sheds $2.7T in June as Investors Run the Capex Math

The Magnificent Seven — plus Broadcom and Oracle — has lost roughly $2.7 trillion in market value during June. Microsoft is on pace for its worst monthly drop since December 2000. The weakness is uniform across the group; in past corrections, one or two names typically drag while the rest hold.

The numbers driving the selloff: the four largest hyperscalers — Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta — are on track to spend $725 billion on capital expenditures in 2026, up 77% from 2025's already-record $410 billion. Despite net income rising ~25% to $506 billion, free cash flow across these companies is projected to drop 91% to about $16 billion. The gap between profit and cash generation is what investors are pricing.

*Sources: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/big-techs-27-trillion-ai-bill-comes-due-chart-of-the-day-100000100.html) · [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/06/60124389/magnificent-seven-selloff-june-mags-etf-outflows) · [Knowledge Hub Media](https://knowledgehubmedia.com/big-techs-2-7-trillion-ai-reckoning-what-investors-need-to-know/)*

### Private AI Funding Keeps Flowing at $337B While Public Markets Retreat

AlphaSense raised $350M at a $7.5B valuation in June, backed by Vitruvian Partners, Accenture Ventures, and J.P. Morgan. Supabase, the open-source developer platform popular with AI app builders, raised $500M led by GIC at a $10.5B valuation. Total 2026 AI startup funding has reached $337B across 395 deals — with Q1 alone breaking every prior venture record at $300B globally.

The public-market selloff and private-market surge are happening at the same time. Institutions are rotating, not retreating.
