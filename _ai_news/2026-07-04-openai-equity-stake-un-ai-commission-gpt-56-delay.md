Washington wants a cut of AI's profits, the UN is about to host the biggest AI governance summit ever assembled, and open-weight models keep closing the gap on the frontier. Here's what mattered in the last day and a half.

## Washington Wants a Piece of AI's Profits

### OpenAI Offers the US Government a 5% Stake Worth $42.6 Billion

The Financial Times reported on July 2 that OpenAI proposed donating a 5% equity stake to a government-seeded "Public Wealth Fund," modeled loosely on Alaska's Permanent Fund, which pays oil-revenue dividends to residents. At OpenAI's $852 billion March valuation, that stake is worth $42.6 billion. Sam Altman has been pitching the idea to Trump since early 2025, and it would need congressional approval to happen.

*Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposed-donating-5-of-its-equity-to-a-us-sovereign-wealth-fund/) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposes-us-government-own-5percent-stake-to-address-political-blowback.html)*

### Bernie Sanders Counters With a 50% Stock Tax

Sanders has proposed a one-time 50% stock tax on large AI companies, with proceeds funding a public AI wealth fund his office estimates could reach $7 trillion. Seen next to that bill, OpenAI's 5% donation looks less generous and more like a preemptive strike. Washington already holds a 10% stake in Intel and takes a cut of Nvidia and AMD's China chip sales, so this kind of deal has precedent.

*Sources: [Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/07/03/us-government-ai-equity-stake/)*

## Frontier Model Race Hits a Government Checkpoint

### GPT-5.6 Is Stuck in a Government-Vetted Preview

OpenAI said on June 26 it would delay GPT-5.6's public launch after the White House invoked a new executive order letting federal officials review "covered frontier models" for up to 30 days before wider release. Officials are approving preview customers for the Sol, Terra, and Luna variants one at a time; only about 20 organizations have access so far. Altman posted on X that safety testing "is not a bad idea," but "I just don't like the idea of the government picking the customers."

*Sources: [ghacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/06/29/openai-delays-gpt-5-6-public-launch-as-us-government-requests-early-access-to-frontier-ai-models/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/26/openai-limits-gpt-5-6-rollout-after-government-request-says-restrictions-shouldnt-be-the-norm/)*

### China's Z.ai Ships an Open-Weight Model That Nearly Matches Claude Opus

Z.ai released GLM-5.2, a 753-billion-parameter model, under an MIT open-source license. It scores 62.1 on SWE-bench Pro against GPT-5.5's 58.6, and hits 74.4% on the long-horizon FrontierSWE benchmark, just behind Claude Opus 4.8's 75.1%. It costs $4.40 per million output tokens, versus $25 for Opus 4.8 and $30 for GPT-5.5.

*Sources: [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/z-ais-open-weights-glm-5-2-beats-gpt-5-5-on-multiple-long-horizon-coding-benchmarks-for-1-6th-the-cost)*

### Google Ships a 4-Second, 3-Cent Image Model

Google launched Nano Banana 2 Lite and Gemini Omni Flash on June 30. Nano Banana 2 Lite generates an image in about four seconds for $0.034. Gemini Omni Flash turns images into 10-second videos at $0.10 per second, editable with plain-language prompts, and both are rolling into Search's AI Mode, the Gemini app, Google Photos, and NotebookLM.

*Sources: [true-tech](https://true-tech.net/google-launches-nano-banana-2-lite-image-model/) · [en.ain.ua](https://en.ain.ua/2026/07/01/google-unveiled-the-nano-banana-2-lite-and-gemini-omni-flash-models/)*

## Global AI Governance Converges on Geneva

### The UN Assembles Its Biggest-Ever AI Power Summit

The UN and the International Telecommunication Union are launching the AI for Good Global Commission, co-chaired by Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff and Rwandan President Paul Kagame. Members include Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Amazon's Andy Jassy, Microsoft's Brad Smith, Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark, and Cohere co-founder Aidan Gomez. The commission holds its first meeting July 8 in Geneva, the same week as the UN's first Global Dialogue on AI Governance (July 6-7) and the ITU AI for Good Summit (July 7-10).

*Sources: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/01/un-ai-commission-ceos-world-leaders) · [Eastern Herald](https://easternherald.com/2026/07/02/un-ai-governance-commission-jensen-huang-jassy-benioff-geneva/) · [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/un-and-itu-launch-ai-for-good-commission-led-by-benioff-and-kagame)*

### The EU Finalizes Its AI Act Rewrite, Pushes Tough Rules to 2027

The Council of the EU gave final approval on June 29 to a package that simplifies and delays parts of the AI Act. High-risk system rules now apply from December 2, 2027 for standalone systems and August 2, 2028 for systems embedded in products. The rewrite also adds a flat ban on AI tools that generate non-consensual intimate images or child sexual abuse material, while cutting the grace period for labeling AI-generated content from six months to three.

*Sources: [Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/)*

## The Enterprise AI Deployment Land Grab

### Microsoft Launches a $2.5B Company to Install AI Inside Your Business

Microsoft is spinning up Frontier Company, a $2.5 billion operation with 6,000 employees who embed directly with customers to build and run AI systems, a model known as forward-deployed engineering. Its first clients are Unilever and Novo Nordisk. Customers keep the output of the work, and the unit will connect Microsoft's own AI tools with rivals' where it makes sense.

*Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/microsoft-launches-its-own-ai-deployment-company-with-2-5-billion-commitment/) · [The Tech Portal](https://thetechportal.com/2026/07/02/microsoft-launches-new-2-5bn-ai-deployment-firm-frontier-company-for-enterprises/)*

### AWS Got There First With $1 Billion, Funded Entirely In-House

Two days before Microsoft's announcement, AWS committed $1 billion of its own balance sheet, no outside investors, to a Forward Deployed Engineering unit. Teams of five to six engineers embed with clients like the NFL and Southwest Airlines for roughly 45-day sprints to ship production AI systems. That's a different bet than OpenAI's $4 billion joint venture or Anthropic's roughly $1.5 billion consortium backed by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs.

*Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/aws-amazon-ai-forward-deployed-engineers.html) · [AWS](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/aws/aws-1-billion-forward-deployed-ai-engineers)*
